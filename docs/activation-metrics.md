# Activation metrics

Activation is the moment a new signup first gets real value from your product. If you can't name that moment as an event in your analytics, you can't improve onboarding on purpose.

## Step 1: name the activation event

Write one sentence: "A user is activated when they ___ within ___ days of signing up." Good examples:

- A project tool: created a project and invited one teammate within 3 days.
- An email API: sent a first email that was delivered within 1 day.
- An analytics tool: installed the snippet and viewed a report with real data within 7 days.

Check it against retention: users who hit the event should keep coming back at a clearly higher rate than users who don't. If they don't, pick a different event.

## Step 2: the four numbers to watch

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Activation rate | activated signups ÷ all signups, per weekly cohort |
| Time to activate | median hours from signup to the activation event |
| Setup drop-off | share of signups who stop at each onboarding step |
| Week-4 retention of activated users | activated users active in week 4 ÷ activated users |

## Step 3: one change per cycle

Change one onboarding step at a time, then compare the next two weekly cohorts with the two before. Remove steps before adding tooltips; most gains come from asking for less up front.
